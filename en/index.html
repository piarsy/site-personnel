B2B commercial and operations executive

You can’t understand a business from just one side.

The same machine can be a sale for the manufacturer and stock that the distributor has to finance.

I’ve worked on both sides, led several businesses and founded my own.

Those experiences taught me to look beyond targets: the resources committed, the promises made to customers and the work of the teams delivering on them.

01 / A change of perspective

I’d led a business. Then I became a student again.

A different position.
A different perspective.

At 14, I helped with farm work and selling at markets. Production on one side. The customer on the other.

At 25, a family opportunity led me to manage three John Deere dealership sites in Normandy.

I then studied for a master’s degree in Operations Management at IAE Caen through continuing education, including time in industrial operations at Renault Trucks.

I had come to know business through sales and distribution. Now I was seeing it from the production side.

I’d already been in charge. I still had a lot to learn.

02 / Experience in practice

What I’ve learned to look for.

The responsibilities changed. Some questions stayed with me.

01

Who is responsible once the deal is signed?

A machine still has to be prepared, delivered and maintained after it is sold. Keeping the promise to the customer depends on people in different roles.

At Holmer France, I was responsible for sales, service, inventory and the subsidiary’s financial management.

Holmer France · Subsidiary operations management

02

More sites don’t automatically make a network.

In a network growing through acquisitions, the sites did not all work in the same way.

I set up a shared space for deal files and margin sheets across the sites. The used-equipment manager approved trade-ins; I handled new-equipment deals.

Growth alone wasn’t enough. We also had to find a way to work together.

Equipment distribution · Working across a network

03

When is it time to close a business that has served a purpose?

I founded PuissanceZ, a Qualiopi-certified training provider, and worked with more than 120 business leaders and teams.

I later decided to close the business after examining the limits of its business model.

PuissanceZ · Founding and running a business
